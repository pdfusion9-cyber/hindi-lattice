The history of science is a history of careful observation. When a new instrument is built, it often reveals something that was not expected. The telescope showed moons around another planet. The microscope showed a world inside a drop of water. Each time, the old explanation had to change. A good theory is one that makes a prediction which could fail. If a prediction survives many tests, we begin to trust it. But trust is not proof, and every theory remains open to revision. Mathematics gives us a language for these ideas. A number can be large or small, exact or approximate. When we measure something, we always measure with some error. The question is whether that error matters for the conclusion we draw. Careful people keep asking this question, and that habit, more than any single discovery, is what moves knowledge forward.
